FROM REQUIREMENTS TO RESULTS

Engineering is more than an idea. It's a process.

Effective product development needs clear requirements, considered design choices, practical implementation and evidence that a solution performs as intended.

THE DESIGN THINKING FLOW

Human-centred. Tested in the real world.

We start with the people and problem, explore possible solutions, then prototype and test to learn what works. The process is iterative: insights can take us back to an earlier stage.

01UNDERSTAND

Empathise

Understand the users, their environment, pain points and real-world needs through conversations, observation and context gathering.

OutputUser needs · observations · stakeholder context
02FRAME

Define

Turn what we learned into a focused problem statement, clear constraints and measurable criteria for success.

OutputProblem statement · requirements · success criteria
03EXPLORE

Ideate

Generate and compare possible approaches before committing to one design. Assess feasibility, trade-offs, risks and scope.

OutputConcepts · alternatives · selected approach
04MAKE

Prototype

Build the right level of prototype — from a CAD model or proof of concept to an integrated physical system — to answer key questions.

OutputModels · mock-ups · functional prototypes
05LEARN

Test

Evaluate the prototype with users or against agreed requirements, capture evidence, identify gaps and refine the solution.

OutputTest findings · issues · next iteration
↺
Iterate, don’t just proceed.

Testing may send us back to empathise, redefine the problem, explore another idea or build a revised prototype. Engineering verification and handover follow according to the project scope.

Engineering note: Design thinking helps us find and shape the right problem and solution. Detailed engineering, verification, regulatory compliance and production qualification are planned separately where required; a prototype is not automatically a certified or production-ready product.
